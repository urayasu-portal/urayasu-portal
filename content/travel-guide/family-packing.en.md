<p>With kids, a Disney trip is half preparation. This page organizes three things from a local's point of view: <strong>what to pack, what you can buy locally, and what to decide at booking time</strong>. Start with your child's age.</p>

<div class="tg-note" style="background:#F0F7FB;border:1px solid #CBE3F2;border-radius:10px;padding:14px 16px;margin:16px 0;line-height:1.9">
<strong>🔍 How old is your child?</strong><br>
<a href="#baby">0–3 (stroller age)</a> / <a href="#kids">4–6 (walks, but tires)</a> / <a href="#school">School age</a>
</div>

<h2 id="baby">Ages 0–3 | The stroller years</h2>

<ul class="lg-art-checklist">
  <li><strong>Consider "rental + baby carrier" instead of bringing a stroller.</strong> The parks rent strollers for a fee (eligible ages and prices on the <a href="https://www.tokyodisneyresort.jp/en/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">official site</a>). Stations have elevators if you bring your own.</li>
  <li><strong>Pack diapers and baby food for the full trip, plus spares.</strong> Hotel shops and convenience stores carry some, but brands and sizes are limited.</li>
  <li><strong>Choose a family-ready hotel.</strong> Crib availability and co-sleeping rules differ by hotel — see the <a href="/en/travel-guide/hotels/kids/">family hotel comparison</a>.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="kids">Ages 4–6 | Walks, but tires</h2>

<ul class="lg-art-checklist">
  <li><strong>Fatigue management matters most.</strong> A hotel you can retreat to mid-afternoon (close to the parks, frequent shuttles) makes the day far easier — see the <a href="/en/travel-guide/hotels/shuttle/">shuttle bus guide</a>.</li>
  <li><strong>One change of clothes and a light poncho.</strong> For splashes and spills. If rain is forecast, see the <a href="/en/travel-guide/rainy-day/">rainy day guide</a>.</li>
  <li><strong>Lost-child plan.</strong> Put a card with your contact details in your child's pocket. The parks have lost-children centers.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="school">School age | Walking on their own</h2>

<ul class="lg-art-checklist">
  <li><strong>A children's transit IC card</strong> if you'll ride trains and buses a lot (<a href="/en/travel-guide/ic-card-payment/">IC card & payment guide</a>).</li>
  <li><strong>A power bank.</strong> A full day on the official app drains phones.</li>
  <li><strong>Queue entertainment</strong> (small games, cards) makes the lines much easier.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="common">All-ages packing checklist</h2>

<ul class="lg-art-checklist">
  <li><strong>Familiar medicines and fever reducers</strong>, plus your travel insurance certificate</li>
  <li><strong>Power bank and charging cables</strong></li>
  <li><strong>Rain gear</strong> (top up locally if needed — see the <a href="/en/travel-guide/rainy-day/">rainy day guide</a>)</li>
  <li><strong>Some cash.</strong> The night-time emergency clinic is cash-only (<a href="/en/travel-guide/kids-emergency/">Kids' Emergency Guide</a>)</li>
</ul>
