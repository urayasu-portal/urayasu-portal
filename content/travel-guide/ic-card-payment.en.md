<p>Getting around Tokyo takes one transit IC card (or a phone setup) — trains, buses and convenience stores all work with a tap. Start with your style.</p>

<h2 id="mobile">Phone: add Suica to Apple Wallet / Google Wallet</h2>

<ul>
  <li>On a supported phone you can <strong>issue a Suica in the app without buying a card</strong> and top it up by credit card. Tap your phone at gates and on buses.</li>
  <li>Set it up before you arrive in Japan — it saves you the airport counter queues.</li>
  <li>Some overseas-issued credit cards don't work for top-ups. If yours fails, fall back to a physical card below.</li>
</ul>

Top up with cash at station ticket machines or convenience-store counters. For children's IC cards, ask at the counter when buying.</p>

<h2 id="cash">Cash-only works too</h2>

<p>Without an IC card you can still ride everything — just buy paper tickets at the machines. But you'll queue at a machine for every transfer, so <strong>for stays of two days or more, an IC card (or phone setup) ends up being the easiest</strong>. Keep some cash on hand regardless: a few situations, like the <a href="/en/travel-guide/kids-emergency/">night-time emergency clinic</a>, are cash-only.</p>
