Twenty-five minutes from Maihama, before most Disney guests have finished breakfast, the lanes of **Tsukiji Outer Market** are already handing out slices of tamagoyaki on skewers, grilling scallops in their shells and serving tuna bowls at tiny counters. The wholesale fish auction moved to Toyosu in 2018, but the **outer market stayed** — about 400 shops in a four-block grid beside Tsukiji Hongwanji temple — and for a traveler based near Tokyo Disney Resort it is the most direct "eat Japan" morning there is: one JR ride to Hatchobori, one subway stop, and you're in it. The catch is the calendar: Tsukiji is a **morning** place and is largely shut on **Sundays, holidays and most Wednesdays**.

## Who is this for?

- You want a **sushi breakfast or early lunch** and the pleasure of eating something straight from a market stall.
- You like markets more than museums and your kids are old enough to stand at a counter (or small enough to carry).
- You're choosing between Tsukiji and [Toyosu Senkyakubanrai](/en/travel-guide/toyosu-senkyakubanrai/): Tsukiji is older, denser, cheaper and morning-only; Toyosu is newer, covered, has an onsen and is open until night.
- You want a visit that is **done by noon**, leaving the afternoon for a park or a nap.

{{< note >}}
**Tsukiji vs Toyosu in one line.** Tuna auctions and the wholesale market: **Toyosu** (lottery-only auction viewing, see [the Toyosu guide](/en/travel-guide/toyosu-senkyakubanrai/)). Street food, sushi counters, knives and dried-goods shops in old lanes: **Tsukiji Outer Market** — this page.
{{< /note >}}

## What to expect

### The lanes: what to eat

Expect **tamagoyaki** (sweet rolled omelette, on a stick or in a sandwich), grilled scallops and eel skewers, uni and crab served on the spot, tuna bowls, strawberry daifuku, dashi broth in a paper cup, and sushi counters ranging from quick 10-piece sets to fine omakase. Prices run from a few hundred yen per skewer to ¥3,000–5,000 for a good sushi set. Lines form at the famous counters by 10:00; the lesser-known ones are just as fresh. The **kitchen-goods shops** (knives engraved with your name, Japanese ceramics) are the souvenir angle.

### Hours and the calendar — read this first

The market association defines the day like this:

| Time | What's happening |
|---|---|
| 6:00–9:00 | Trade buyers' priority time — some shops open, but you are in the way |
| **9:00–14:00** | **Regular retail hours — the time to visit** |
| 14:00 onward | Shops close one by one |

**Closed days follow the wholesale market calendar**: Sundays, public holidays and most Wednesdays, when many — not all — shops shut. For October–December 2026 the seafood-market closures are, for example, **October 18, 21, 25, 28; November 1, 3, 8, 11, 15, 18, 22, 23, 29; December 2, 6, 9, 13, 16, 20, 23 and 31**. Some restaurants open anyway, but the lanes lose their point. Check the official calendar when you fix the date, and aim for **9:00–11:00** on a Tuesday, Thursday, Friday or Saturday.

### The rules (official, and they mean them)

1. **No eating while walking** — eat at the counter or in front of the shop.
2. Don't touch the displayed food.
3. Ask before photographing shops, staff or other customers.
4. Big bags into coin lockers; **strollers can be left at Platt Tsukiji** (the information center).
5. **No haggling** — prices are fixed.
6. Smoking only in designated areas.

### Platt Tsukiji — information, lockers, ATM, tax-free

The information center on the main lane opens **8:00–14:00 on weekdays and Saturdays (10:00–14:00 on Sundays, holidays and market closed days)** with English-speaking staff, coin lockers, a Seven Bank ATM, a currency-exchange machine and a tax-free counter. It is the place to park the stroller and the bags. (Irregular closures are posted on the official site — October 7, 21 and 28, 2026 were listed.)

### Tsukiji Hongwanji — the temple next door

The large stone temple with the Indian-style dome at the Tsukiji Station end of the market is **Tsukiji Hongwanji**: main hall **6:00–16:00**, no admission charge, connected directly to Hibiya Line exit 1. Its café **Tsumugi (8:00–18:00)** serves a famous multi-dish Japanese breakfast from 8:00–10:30 — the calm way to start before the lanes fill. The temple has no fortune slips or charms; it is a place to sit for ten minutes.

### The construction site next door

The former inner market — the huge fenced area between the outer market and the river — is being redeveloped by a consortium led by Mitsui Fudosan into a district with a large stadium, offices and waterfront facilities, with the first areas due in the early 2030s. In 2026 there is nothing to see there; don't wander over looking for the old auction halls.

## How to get there from the Tokyo Disney Resort area

**From Maihama:**

1. **JR Keiyo Line** to **Hatchobori** — about 10 minutes by rapid, 14 by local, IC ¥253.
2. Walk through the station to the **Hibiya Line** (about 5–8 minutes between the platforms) and ride **one stop to Tsukiji** — ¥178.
3. Take **exit 1 or 2**; the market lanes start a minute away, and Tsukiji Hongwanji is at exit 1.

**About 25 minutes and ¥431 one way.** Alternative: Keiyo Line to Shin-Kiba, Yurakucho Line to Shintomicho, and a 10-minute walk — ¥377, about 30 minutes.

**From Urayasu Station (Tozai Line):** Tozai Line to **Kayabacho**, Hibiya Line two stops to **Tsukiji** — about 25 minutes, one transfer inside the gates, IC ¥252.

**From Shin-Urayasu:** add one Keiyo Line stop (3 minutes) to the Maihama route.

Suica/PASMO works on all of the above. Contactless credit cards work on the Tokyo Metro part but not on the JR Keiyo Line.

## With kids, and when it rains

- **Strollers:** leave them at Platt Tsukiji and carry toddlers; the lanes are shoulder-to-shoulder from 10:30.
- **Food for kids:** tamagoyaki, grilled rice balls, fruit skewers and strawberry daifuku are safe bets; many sushi counters have no children's menu.
- **Cash:** many stalls prefer cash; card acceptance varies by shop. There is an ATM at Platt Tsukiji.
- **Rain:** parts of the lanes are under arcades, but it is mostly outdoors — on a wet forecast, [Toyosu Senkyakubanrai](/en/travel-guide/toyosu-senkyakubanrai/) (covered, with an onsen) is the better market.

## Pair it with

- **Ginza** is one Hibiya Line stop away (or a 15-minute walk) for department-store basements and the Pokémon/Nintendo/Uniqlo flagship cluster — a natural afternoon.
- [Toyosu Senkyakubanrai and the 24-hour onsen](/en/travel-guide/toyosu-senkyakubanrai/) — the "other" market, on the way home: Yurakucho Line from Shintomicho to Toyosu.
- [Kiyosumi Garden](/en/travel-guide/kiyosumi-garden/) — a 10-minute Oedo Line ride (Tsukiji-shijo → Kiyosumi-Shirakawa) to a quiet garden and coffee for the afternoon.
