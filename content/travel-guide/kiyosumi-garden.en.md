Not every rest day needs an attraction. If what your family needs is **two hours of quiet** — a pond with carp, stepping stones, a cup of properly made coffee and a small museum — the place is **Kiyosumi-Shirakawa**, an old canal-side neighbourhood in Koto Ward that is, by Tokyo luck, **20 minutes and one easy transfer from Urayasu Station**. Its centrepiece is **Kiyosumi Garden**, a Meiji-era stroll garden that costs ¥150 to enter and is free for children; around it are the roasteries that made the area Tokyo's coffee town (Blue Bottle's first Japanese café is here), the Museum of Contemporary Art, and the Fukagawa Edo Museum, where kids can walk into a reconstructed 1840s street.

## Who is this for?

- Adults who've done two park days and want to **sit down somewhere beautiful** without a queue.
- Families with toddlers who are happy with ducks, stepping stones and a cake, and need naps to be possible.
- Coffee people.
- A gentle "Japanese garden" experience without the tour-bus crowds of the famous central gardens.

{{< note >}}
**Which kind of "Japanese garden" is this?** A *kaiyu* (stroll) garden — a path around a large pond with islands, stones brought from all over Japan, a teahouse pavilion over the water, and seasonal flowers. It is neither a Zen rock garden nor a flower park; the pleasure is in walking slowly. Allow 45–60 minutes.
{{< /note >}}

## What to see and do

### Kiyosumi Garden (¥150, kids free)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Hours** | 9:00–17:00 (last entry 16:30) |
| **Closed** | December 29 – January 1 |
| **Admission** | Adults ¥150 / 65+ ¥70 / elementary age and under free (junior-high students living or studying in Tokyo free) |
| **Free days** | May 4 and October 1 |
| **Access** | 3 minutes from Kiyosumi-Shirakawa Station (Toei Oedo Line E14 / Tokyo Metro Hanzomon Line Z11) |
| **Notes** | No pets; no parking; wheelchair route map available |

The route around the pond passes the **stepping-stone paths (*isowatari*)** that let you walk a few metres out over the water with carp and turtles underneath — the part children remember — and the **Ryotei** pavilion extending over the pond. There are benches in the shade and a lawn area. Autumn colour arrives in late November; in summer it is one of the few central gardens with real shade.

### Coffee: the roaster neighbourhood

The streets between the garden and the museum of contemporary art are where Tokyo's third-wave coffee started. The anchor is **Blue Bottle Coffee Kiyosumi Shirakawa Flagship Cafe** (1-4-8 Hirano, **8:00–19:00 daily**, 47 seats, Wi-Fi), a converted warehouse that opened as Blue Bottle's first café outside the US in 2015. Several small independent roasters are within a few blocks; most open mid-morning and close by 18:00, and several close on Mondays or irregularly — treat anything but Blue Bottle's hours as "check on the day". With kids, the trick is take-out coffee and a bench in the garden, or the roomier cafés along Kiyosumi-dori.

### Fukagawa Edo Museum (the one for kids)

Five minutes from the station (exit A3): a full-scale, walk-in reconstruction of a canal-side neighbourhood of 1840s Edo — tenement houses you step into, a vegetable shop, a boat, a fire tower, with the lighting cycling from dawn to night. **9:30–17:00 (entry to 16:30), closed on the 2nd and 4th Mondays; adults ¥400, elementary to high-school students ¥50.** Volunteer guides often explain in English. About 45 minutes; children can touch most things.

### Museum of Contemporary Art Tokyo (MOT)

A 9-minute walk from the station's B2 exit, in Kiba Park: a large contemporary art museum with a good library, a café and lawns outside. **10:00–18:00 (entry to 17:30), closed Mondays (or the next day after a holiday Monday) and over the New Year; collection exhibition ¥500 for adults, ¥400 university, ¥250 high school and 65+, junior high and younger free.** Special exhibitions are priced separately. Check the closure calendar — it closes for exhibition changeovers (several weeks in January–February 2027).

## How to get there from the Tokyo Disney Resort area

**From Urayasu Station (Tozai Line) — the easy one:**

1. **Tozai Line** to **Monzen-nakacho** (2 stops by rapid train, 6 by local; about 11–14 minutes, ¥209).
2. **Toei Oedo Line** 1 stop to **Kiyosumi-Shirakawa** (about 2 minutes).

**About 20 minutes, IC ¥317** (the ¥70 Metro–Toei transfer discount is applied automatically with an IC card), one transfer. The garden is 3 minutes from exit A3.

**From Maihama (Keiyo Line):**

1. **JR Keiyo Line** to **Shin-Kiba** (about 6 minutes, ¥199).
2. **Yurakucho Line** to **Tsukishima** (about 7 minutes).
3. **Oedo Line** 2 stops to **Kiyosumi-Shirakawa**.

**About 35 minutes, ¥485**, two transfers. The Metro-only alternative via Hatchobori (Hibiya Line to Ningyocho, walk to Suitengumae, Hanzomon Line one stop) is ¥431 and about the same time but involves a street-level walk between stations.

**From Shin-Urayasu:** the bus to Urayasu Station and the Tozai route is often simpler than the Keiyo route — see [bus stops by station](/en/travel-guide/station-bus-stops/).

Suica/PASMO works everywhere above; contactless credit cards work on Tokyo Metro and Toei but not on the JR Keiyo Line.

## A slow half-day plan

| Time | What |
|---|---|
| 9:30 | Leave Urayasu Station; coffee and a pastry at a roaster by 10:00 |
| 10:30 | Kiyosumi Garden — one slow lap, stepping stones, benches (45–60 minutes) |
| 11:45 | Fukagawa Edo Museum (kids) or MOT (teens and adults) |
| 13:00 | Lunch on Kiyosumi-dori — soba, curry and bakeries are the local options |
| 14:30 | Back to the hotel for a nap, or two Oedo Line stops to [Ryogoku](/en/travel-guide/ryogoku-sumo/), or four to [Tsukiji](/en/travel-guide/tsukiji-outer-market/) |

## With kids, and when it rains

- **Strollers:** fine on the main gravel loop of the garden, not on the stepping stones; the museums both have elevators and the Fukagawa museum is single-level inside.
- **Restrooms:** in the garden near the entrance and in both museums.
- **Rain:** the garden is quiet and atmospheric in light rain and the two museums cover a wet afternoon; heavy rain makes the gravel paths puddle — bring the stroller rain cover.
- **Food:** most cafés are small; weekend afternoons fill up. Lunch early.
