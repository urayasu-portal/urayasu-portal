If your family came to Japan for Disney but wants one morning that looks like the Japan in the picture books — a red lantern gate, a smoky incense cauldron, a lane of paper fans and rice crackers, people in kimono — that morning is **Asakusa**. It is also, by Tokyo standards, easy from the Disney area: **about 35 minutes and ¥252 from Urayasu Station** with a single subway transfer, or about 40 minutes from Maihama. This guide is the practical version: what is actually free, when to arrive, how the kimono and tea-ceremony add-ons work with children, and how to connect it to Skytree or a river boat to Odaiba.

## Who is this for?

- First-time visitors who want **Senso-ji and Kaminarimon** in the memory bank — it is the standard "old Tokyo" photo for a reason.
- Families with kids over about 5 who can handle a crowded street for an hour and be rewarded with snacks.
- Anyone planning a **kimono rental** or **tea ceremony** day: both are concentrated within a few minutes of the gate.
- Travelers who'd like to arrive or leave **by boat** on the Sumida River.

## What to see and do

### Senso-ji, Kaminarimon and Hozomon

Tokyo's oldest temple complex. You enter through **Kaminarimon** (the Thunder Gate with the huge red lantern), walk the 250-metre **Nakamise** shopping lane, pass the second gate (Hozomon) and reach the main hall, with the five-storey pagoda to your left. **There is no admission charge for the grounds.** The **main hall is open 6:00–17:00 (from 6:30 in October–March)**; the grounds themselves don't have gates, and the buildings are lit up after dark, which is the quietest and most photogenic time. Draw an *omikuji* fortune (¥100), waft incense smoke over yourself at the cauldron, and look for the small Asakusa Shrine to the right of the main hall.

### Nakamise: 87 shops of snacks and souvenirs

The lane between the two gates has **87 shops** — hours vary by shop and are not published centrally; most are open roughly mid-morning to early evening. Highlights for kids: freshly made *ningyo-yaki* (little sponge cakes), *kibi-dango*, giant rice crackers, melon pan, and fan and toy shops. Eating while walking is frowned upon on Nakamise; step aside to the shop front. Side streets like Denboin-dori are calmer and have the same snacks.

### Asakusa Culture Tourist Information Center — free view and stroller rental

Directly opposite Kaminarimon is Taito City's tourist center (the striking stacked-wooden building): **multilingual information (Japanese, English, Chinese, Korean) 9:00–20:00**, restrooms, a café, **stroller and wheelchair rental 9:00–19:30**, and — the local tip — a **free 8th-floor observation terrace open 9:00–22:00** with a straight view down Nakamise to the temple and across to Tokyo Skytree. Go up first to understand the layout.

### Kimono rental (from about ¥3,300)

A dozen rental shops cluster within three minutes of Kaminarimon. Three with clear official information:

| Shop | Where | From | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **VASARA** | Three Asakusa shops (main shop at Asakusa 1-1-11), 9:00–18:00 | Standard plan ¥3,278 | Big chain; online booking; hair styling extra |
| **Kyoto Kimono Rental wargo** | Asakusa 2-6-7, 4F; 10:00–18:00, last dressing 17:30 | see shop page | Website in 12 languages; 1 minute from the station |
| **Aiwafuku** | Kaminarimon 2-17-9, 4F, and a main shop at Hanakawado | Student plan ¥3,300, couple plan ¥7,300 (2 people) | English- and Chinese-speaking staff; last check-in 15:00; return by 16:30–17:30; same-day booking possible |

Realistic timing with children: dressing takes 20–40 minutes per person, you'll walk slowly in zori sandals, and everything must be back by late afternoon — so **book a morning slot, wear the kimono for 2–3 hours around the temple, and return it before lunch or straight after**. Children's kimono are available at most shops; toddlers in kimono last about 30 minutes.

### Tea ceremony and sweets (45–90 minutes)

- **Chazen Asakusa** (2 minutes from Kaminarimon): a 45-minute tea ceremony in Japanese and English, **¥3,500 per person**, sessions on the hour 10:00–17:00, booking by web form with advance payment. A private session is ¥5,000 per person (minimum 2).
- **MAIKOYA Asakusa**: casual-clothes tea ceremony (45 minutes, English instructor) and longer kimono-plus-tea or sweets-making sessions; **children under 7 are not admitted**, so this one suits parents taking turns or families with older kids. Prices are on the official English site.

### The river boat to Odaiba

From the pier beside Azuma Bridge (1 minute from the Ginza Line station), **Tokyo Cruise** water buses run down the Sumida River: to **Hinode Pier ¥1,000** (about 40 minutes), to **Hamarikyu Gardens ¥1,180** including garden entry, and **direct to Odaiba Marine Park ¥2,000** (50–65 minutes depending on the boat). Children 6–11 half price; one infant per adult free. It is a scenic, seat-guaranteed way to finish an Asakusa morning at the [Odaiba fountain show](/en/travel-guide/odaiba/) — and from Odaiba you are 30 minutes from Maihama.

## How to get there from the Tokyo Disney Resort area

**From Urayasu Station (Tozai Line) — the easy one:**

1. **Tozai Line** to **Nihombashi** — about 16–18 minutes.
2. **Ginza Line** to **Asakusa** (terminus) — about 14 minutes.

**About 35 minutes, IC ¥252, one transfer** inside the same ticket gates. Exit 1 of the Ginza Line station brings you up a minute from Kaminarimon. (Swapping to the Toei Asakusa Line at Nihombashi is a few minutes faster but ¥359 and a longer walk — exit A4.)

**From Maihama (Keiyo Line):**

1. **JR Keiyo Line** to **Hatchobori** — about 10–14 minutes, ¥253.
2. **Hibiya Line** to **Ueno** — about 10 minutes.
3. **Ginza Line** to **Asakusa** — 3 stops, about 5 minutes.

**About 40 minutes, ¥462, two transfers.** The faster variant (Hibiya Line to Ningyocho, then the Toei Asakusa Line) is about 33 minutes but ¥539. **Do not route via Tokyo Station** — Tokyo Station is not on the Toei Asakusa Line, and the Keiyo platforms add a 15-minute walk to any subway there.

**Hotel on the Keiyo side but want the cheap route?** A taxi or bus from Maihama to Urayasu Station (about 20 minutes) and the Tozai route costs about the same as the Keiyo route and avoids the double transfer — see the [bus stops by station](/en/travel-guide/station-bus-stops/).

Suica/PASMO works everywhere above; contactless credit cards work on Tokyo Metro and Toei but not on the JR Keiyo Line.

## A half-day plan (temple first, snacks second)

| Time | What |
|---|---|
| 8:30 | Leave the hotel; arrive Asakusa around 9:15, before the tour groups |
| 9:15 | Kaminarimon photo while the lane is still quiet; walk Nakamise to the main hall |
| 9:45 | Senso-ji: incense, omikuji, Asakusa Shrine; pagoda photo |
| 10:30 | Up to the 8F terrace of the tourist center for the view, restroom, stroller return |
| 11:00 | Snacks on Nakamise and Denboin-dori; or a kimono/tea slot booked for 10:00–12:00 |
| 12:30 | Lunch (tempura, soba and *monjayaki* are the local classics), then either the 20-minute walk over the Sumida River Walk to [Tokyo Skytree](/en/travel-guide/tokyo-skytree/), or the boat to Odaiba |

Arrive after 11:00 on a weekend and the lane is a slow-moving crowd — go early, or go late afternoon when shops are closing but the lights come on.

## With kids, and when it rains

- **Strollers:** fine on Nakamise (flat) but slow in the crowd; the temple grounds are gravel with steps up to the main hall. Rent one at the tourist center if you'd rather not bring yours.
- **Restrooms:** at the tourist center and on the temple grounds.
- **Rain:** the temple and the lane are outdoors; the arcade streets west of the temple (Shin-Nakamise) are covered and there is the tourist center, but Asakusa is a sunny-day plan. In rain, swap to [Tokyo Character Street](/en/travel-guide/tokyo-character-street/) or [SMALL WORLDS](/en/travel-guide/small-worlds/).
- Tempura and soba restaurants are child-friendly; look for *oko-sama* (children's) sets.
