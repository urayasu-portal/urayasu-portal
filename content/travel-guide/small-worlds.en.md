One of the best-kept secrets for families staying near Tokyo Disney Resort sits in a warehouse-style building in Ariake, **about 25 minutes from Maihama**: **SMALL WORLDS**, one of the largest miniature-world museums in Asia. Inside, tiny rockets launch from a space center, a 1/80-scale Kansai International Airport runs flights, trains wind through a global village, and Evangelion units stand in their hangars — with day turning to night every few minutes. It is **completely indoors**, open **9:00–19:00 every day** this autumn, and takes about two hours, which makes it the easiest answer to "it's raining and the kids are bouncing off the hotel walls".

## Who is this for?

- Families with children roughly **4–12** who like trains, planes, LEGO-scale detail and pressing buttons.
- A **rainy or extremely hot day** when you want guaranteed indoor time without a teamLab-style sell-out risk.
- Anime fans: the two **Evangelion** areas and the new **Shibuya** area are a draw in themselves.
- Anyone who wants to **become a miniature** — a 3D scan of yourself turned into a figure, shipped home.

{{< note >}}
**On the "is it closed?" question.** The original operating company went into bankruptcy proceedings in late 2023 after handing the business to a new company, SMALL WORLDS Inc., which runs it today. As of October 2026 the museum posts daily notices, the calendar shows no closed days through November, and a Shibuya area opened in June 2026. Ignore 2023-era articles saying it shut down.
{{< /note >}}

## What to expect

### Nine areas, one giant diorama

The current official list of areas: **Space Center** (rocket launches — the launch show was temporarily suspended for maintenance at the time of writing, so check the news page), **Diaclone**, **Global Village** (streets of the world at 1/80 scale, with trains), **Creators' Gallery**, the **Shibuya Area** (opened June 11, 2026 — the scramble crossing in miniature), **Kansai International Airport** (planes taxi and take off), **Evangelion Hangar** and **Evangelion Tokyo-III**, plus an **Ariake Arena** display. The Sailor Moon area that older guides mention is no longer on the official list.

Each area cycles through a day-night lighting sequence, so stand still for a few minutes and the scene changes. Buttons and viewing steps are at child height in many spots.

### Make yourself tiny

The signature extra is the **3D figure**: you are scanned on the 3rd floor and a miniature of you is made (1/35 scale from ¥6,600; larger sizes up to ¥24,800) and mailed to you in about 30 days — international shipping conditions are on the official page. There are also **workshops** (weekdays 12:00–16:00, weekends and holidays 10:00–16:45) where children build or paint small pieces. A café (10:30–18:00) is on site.

## How to get there from the Tokyo Disney Resort area

**From Maihama (fastest):**

1. **JR Keiyo Line** to **Shin-Kiba** — 2 stops, about 6 minutes, IC ¥199.
2. **Rinkai Line** one stop to **Kokusai-Tenjijo** — about 5 minutes, ¥272.
3. Walk about **9 minutes** (flat, wide sidewalks; stroller-friendly).

**About 25 minutes and ¥471 one way.** From Shin-Urayasu add one Keiyo Line stop (3 minutes).

**Via the Yurikamome (closest station):** Keiyo Line to Shin-Kiba, Yurakucho Line to Toyosu (¥178), Yurikamome to **Ariake-Tennis-no-mori** (¥189, about 5 minutes) — a **3-minute walk** to the door. Slightly longer and ¥566, but the shortest walk, and the same Yurikamome line continues to Odaiba.

**From Urayasu Station (Tozai Line):** the practical route is a bus or taxi to Shin-Urayasu or Maihama and the Keiyo Line as above — there is no quick rail link from the Tozai Line to Ariake.

Suica/PASMO works on all of these lines. Contactless credit-card tap-in is **not** accepted on the JR Keiyo Line or the Rinkai Line — see the [IC card guide](/en/travel-guide/ic-card-payment/).

## With kids

- **Strollers** are allowed throughout; there is one loaner wheelchair at the 1F counter. Nursing rooms and accessible toilets are on the 2nd and 3rd floors.
- Displays sit roughly at adult chest height. Toddlers see best from a shoulder or carrier; there are step stools at some windows.
- Budget for the gift shop and the capsule machines near the exit — the miniature-figure souvenirs are hard to walk past.
- Average visit is two hours; with a four-year-old, plan 90 minutes and the café.

## Pair it with

- [Odaiba: the free fountain show and the bay](/en/travel-guide/odaiba/) — one Rinkai Line stop (Tokyo Teleport) or a 20-minute walk; SMALL WORLDS in the morning, fountain at 15:00.
- [teamLab Planets](/en/travel-guide/teamlab-planets/) and [Toyosu Senkyakubanrai](/en/travel-guide/toyosu-senkyakubanrai/) — two Yurikamome stops away toward Toyosu. A full indoor day is possible, but two ticketed museums plus a market lunch is a lot for small legs — pick one museum.
