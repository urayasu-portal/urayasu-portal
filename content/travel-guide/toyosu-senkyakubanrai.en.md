Walk out of Shijo-mae Station on the Yurikamome and the deck leads straight into a lantern-lit lane of wooden shopfronts: **Toyosu Senkyakubanrai (豊洲 千客万来)**, the Edo-themed food-and-spa complex beside Tokyo's main fish market. For anyone staying near Tokyo Disney Resort it does two things most Tokyo "foodie" spots don't: it is **about 30 minutes from Maihama via Shin-Kiba**, and after you've eaten too much tuna it offers a **free rooftop footbath** and a **24-hour hot spring** to put your park-weary legs in. It is also a 10-minute walk from teamLab Planets, which is why locals treat the two as one outing.

## Who is this for?

- You want to eat Japanese seafood properly once on this trip — sushi, kaisen-don, grilled scallops, tamagoyaki on a stick — without a 5 a.m. alarm.
- You're pairing it with [teamLab Planets](/en/travel-guide/teamlab-planets/) and need a lunch-and-recovery stop within walking distance.
- You'd like an onsen that is bigger than the hotel bath, open at any hour, and reachable by train.
- You have kids who will eat a skewer of something while you queue for sushi.

{{< note >}}
**Name check.** The food-hall part of the complex was called "Toyosu Jogai Edomae Ichiba" until September 1, 2025, when the whole site took the name **Toyosu Senkyakubanrai**. Signs, maps and older blog posts use both names; they are the same place. The wholesale **Toyosu Market** next door is a separate facility with its own (limited) visitor areas.
{{< /note >}}

## What to expect

### Edo-style streets of seafood (2F–3F)

The main building recreates an Edo-period merchant street in timber and white plaster, two and three storeys high, lined with sushi counters, seafood-bowl shops, grilled-seafood stalls, tamagoyaki, dashi, Japanese sweets and souvenir shops. You eat standing at counters, at small tables, or on the third-floor terrace. Prices run from a few hundred yen for a skewer or a sweet to the usual Tokyo sushi-counter range; a seafood bowl in the ¥2,000–4,000 band is typical for the market area.

**Hours:** shops **10:00–18:00**; restaurants **10:00–22:00** (some close at 20:00; a few open from 9:00). **Open year-round**, including market closed days. There is free Wi-Fi (SSID "SENKYAKU_BANRAI"). Card, smartphone and IC payment are accepted "depending on the shop" — carry some cash for the stalls.

### The free Footbath Garden (8F)

Up the back of the 2nd floor is the **Footbath Garden**: a landscaped rooftop with long footbaths, views over the market cranes and Tokyo Bay, and benches for the person holding the ice cream. **Free, no spa ticket needed, 9:00–22:00.** It is the single best 20 minutes you can give feet that walked 20,000 steps yesterday. Bring a small towel (or buy one); hours are shortened on some event days, and it closes entirely on fireworks day (October 24, 2026).

### Tokyo Toyosu Manyo Club — the 24-hour hot spring (the tall building)

The spa tower is **open 24 hours, year-round**, with baths cleaned 3:00–5:00. The standard day set — **adults ¥3,850 (junior-high age and up) + ¥150 bathing tax**, includes yukata, bath towel and hand towel, so you can arrive with nothing. Children pay less (elementary ¥2,000; ages 3–5 ¥1,400; under 3 free, per the 2026 price list — check the official page before you go). Inside: large indoor and open-air baths with water trucked from Hakone and Yugawara, an observation footbath (10F, spa customers only), a relaxation floor with reclining chairs, restaurants, and **private family baths at ¥4,500 per hour** if tattoos or shy kids make the public baths awkward. A late-night surcharge applies after 3:00, and overnight rooms exist if you ever need a bed in Toyosu.

Tattoo policy and towel details are on the official site; like most Japanese spas, visible tattoos can be an issue — the private family bath is the workaround.

{{< warn >}}
**Toyosu Market's tuna auction is not part of this complex.** Auction viewing from the deck is free but **by advance lottery**, applied for monthly online, with a 5:30 meet-up; the general 2nd-floor viewing corridor (5:00–17:00) is closed on market holidays — Sundays, public holidays and most Wednesdays. If the auction matters to you, plan it separately and expect an early morning.
{{< /warn >}}

## How to get there from the Tokyo Disney Resort area

**From Maihama:**

1. **JR Keiyo Line** to **Shin-Kiba** — 2 stops, about 6 minutes, IC ¥199.
2. **Tokyo Metro Yurakucho Line** to **Toyosu** — 2 stops, about 5 minutes, ¥178.
3. **Yurikamome** one stop to **Shijo-mae** — 3 minutes, ¥189. The station's 2nd-floor deck connects directly to the complex (about a 4-minute walk).

**About 30 minutes and ¥566 one way** with two short transfers, all underground or covered. From Shin-Urayasu add one Keiyo Line stop (3 minutes).

**Skipping the Yurikamome:** from Toyosu Station it is about a 20-minute walk — fine on a nice day, long with a stroller after a market lunch.

**From Urayasu Station (Tozai Line):** Monzen-nakacho is close to Toyosu, but there is no direct rail link; a taxi from Monzen-nakacho (about 10 minutes) or the Tozai → Yurakucho Line via Iidabashi route (long) are the options. If your hotel is near Urayasu Station, this is a day where the Keiyo Line from Shin-Urayasu is worth the bus ride.

Suica/PASMO works on all three lines. The Yurikamome accepts contactless credit-card tap-in, but the JR Keiyo Line does not — see the [IC card guide](/en/travel-guide/ic-card-payment/).

## A half-day plan (with teamLab)

| Time | What |
|---|---|
| 10:00 | teamLab Planets with a morning timed ticket (2–3 hours, barefoot — see [the teamLab guide](/en/travel-guide/teamlab-planets/)) |
| 13:00 | Walk about 10 minutes (or one Yurikamome stop) to Senkyakubanrai; late lunch after the 12:00 rush eases |
| 14:30 | Snacks and souvenirs on the 2nd floor — tamagoyaki, dashi, dried seafood |
| 15:00 | Free footbath on 8F, 20–30 minutes |
| 15:45 | Optional: Manyo Club bath (2 hours) — or head home and soak at [Maihama Eurasia](/en/travel-guide/maihama-eurasia-spa/) instead |
| 18:00 | Back in Maihama |

Without teamLab, arrive at opening (10:00) for the shortest sushi queues, and the plan fits comfortably before an afternoon park return.

## With kids, and when it rains

- **Strollers** are allowed (no stroller parking is provided, so expect to push it through the lanes). Elevators connect the floors; multi-purpose toilets are on 1F–3F and **nursing rooms on the 2nd floor of the spa building and the 3rd floor** of the market building.
- **Picky eaters:** tamagoyaki, grilled rice crackers, melon pan, soft-serve and skewers are everywhere; there is also a seafood buffet restaurant on 3F for a sit-down meal with choices.
- **Rain:** the lanes are covered and the spa is indoors; only the footbath and terrace are open-air (the footbath is still usable in light rain). It is a good rainy-day pairing with teamLab.
- **Crowds:** weekends around noon are packed; weekday opening time is calm.
