Sumo is the one Japanese spectacle most visitors assume they'll never see — and **Ryogoku**, Tokyo's sumo town, makes it easy to get close even when there is no tournament: the **Kokugikan** arena with its free **Sumo Museum**, wrestlers in yukata on the street, **chanko** hot-pot restaurants, and since **March 31, 2026** the big one — the **Edo-Tokyo Museum**, reopened after a four-year renovation, with its full-scale Edo bridge and kabuki theatre reconstruction. For a family based near Tokyo Disney Resort it is a compact, mostly **indoor** half day about 40 minutes away, and it pairs naturally with [Asakusa](/en/travel-guide/asakusa/) and [Tokyo Skytree](/en/travel-guide/tokyo-skytree/) on the same side of the river.

## Who is this for?

- Anyone curious about **sumo** — the museum and the neighbourhood deliver even without a ticket.
- Families with school-age kids: the Edo-Tokyo Museum is built for walking into (and onto) life-size models, and **kids up to junior-high age enter free**.
- A **rainy day** that still feels like "Japan" rather than a mall.
- Art and history travelers who want the Hokusai Museum and an old-Tokyo lunch.

{{< note >}}
**2026–27 tournament dates (Tokyo only):** January 10–24, 2027 (advance tickets on sale from **December 5, 2026**), May 9–23, 2027, September 12–26, 2027. The November 2026 tournament (November 8–22) is in **Fukuoka**, not Tokyo. If your trip overlaps a Tokyo tournament, buy tickets on the official Sumo Association site the day sales open — weekends sell out.

## What to see and do

### Edo-Tokyo Museum (reopened March 31, 2026)

The renovated museum reopened with a full-size replica of the Nihonbashi bridge you walk across, reconstructed Edo-period townhouses and a kabuki theatre façade, interactive exhibits on the city's history from samurai times to the 1964 Olympics, and a new walkway straight from the Oedo Line station. **Hours 9:30–17:30 (Saturdays 9:30–19:30; November Fridays to 20:00), last entry 30 minutes before closing; closed Mondays** (or the following day when Monday is a holiday) and over the New Year. **Permanent exhibition: adults ¥800, 65+ ¥400, university ¥480, high school ¥300, junior high and younger free.** Special exhibitions cost extra (¥2,100 for the autumn 2026 show). A free multilingual digital guide (via the Bloomberg Connects app) and audio guides are available. Plan 2 hours; with children, the big walk-in models alone fill an hour.

### Sumo Museum (free) and the Kokugikan

Inside the Kokugikan's ground floor, the small **Sumo Museum** shows champions' portraits, ceremonial aprons, woodblock prints and changing exhibitions. **Free. Weekdays 10:00–16:30 (last entry 16:00); closed Saturdays, Sundays, holidays and extra days listed on the monthly calendar** (in November 2026, for example, it is closed November 1, 3–8, 14–15, 20–23 and 28–29). During Tokyo tournaments it opens 12:30–16:00 and **requires a tournament ticket**. The arena floor is not open for general tours; the museum, the banners outside and the wrestler statues along the station road are the no-ticket experience. Two minutes from the JR west exit.

### Chanko and -Ryogoku Edo NOREN-

**Chanko-nabe**, the protein-rich hot pot wrestlers eat, is the meal to have here. The easiest place is **-Ryogoku Edo NOREN-**, the restaurant complex built into the old station building at the JR west exit: a dozen restaurants around a mini sumo ring, **10:00–23:00** (closed January 1–2 and on inspection days), including a chanko restaurant run by a former wrestler's family. Lunch sets are the affordable entry point; dinner chanko for two to share is typically ¥3,000–5,000 per person. Chanko restaurants run by former wrestlers line the streets south of the station too.

### Sumida Hokusai Museum

A striking silver building 5 minutes from the Oedo Line's A3 exit, devoted to the artist of *The Great Wave*, who was born here. **9:30–17:30, closed Mondays, permanent exhibition ¥400 (junior high and younger free)**; special exhibitions extra. Compact (45 minutes) and child-tolerant, with touch screens that zoom into the prints.

## How to get there from the Tokyo Disney Resort area

**From Urayasu Station (Tozai Line) — fastest:**

1. **Tozai Line** to **Monzen-nakacho** (2 stops by rapid train, 6 by local; about 11–14 minutes).
2. **Toei Oedo Line** 3 stops to **Ryogoku** (about 6 minutes).
3. Exit A3: Hokusai Museum 5 minutes, Kokugikan about 9 minutes; the new walkway leads to the Edo-Tokyo Museum.

**About 25–30 minutes, IC ¥317** (the Metro–Toei transfer discount is included), one transfer.

**From Maihama (Keiyo Line) — cheapest is all-JR:**

1. **JR Keiyo Line** to **Tokyo** — about 12–16 minutes.
2. **Keihin-Tohoku or Yamanote Line** 2 stops to **Akihabara** (or Chuo rapid to Ochanomizu).
3. **Sobu local line** 2 stops to **Ryogoku** — the west exit is the sumo side.

**About 42 minutes, IC ¥341**, two transfers — but the walk from the Keiyo platforms to the Yamanote platforms at Tokyo Station is 10–15 minutes, so budget for it. The faster variant (Keiyo to Hatchobori, Hibiya Line to Akihabara, Sobu local to Ryogoku) is about 37 minutes but ¥586.

**From Asakusa:** Toei Asakusa Line one stop to Kuramae, then the Oedo Line one stop to Ryogoku — about 15 minutes; or a 30-minute walk down the river. **From Tokyo Skytree:** Oshiage to Ryogoku is 10 minutes by the Oedo Line via Kuramae, or a Tobu + JR hop.

Suica/PASMO works on all routes; contactless credit cards work on Tokyo Metro and Toei lines but not on JR.

## With kids, and when it rains

- The Edo-Tokyo Museum is stroller-friendly with elevators, nursing rooms and lockers; the models are climb-on-able in places, which is the point.
- Chanko is mild and kid-friendly (chicken broth, vegetables, dumplings); most restaurants serve rice and small portions.
- Rain is no problem: JR Ryogoku → Edo NOREN is direct, the Kokugikan is 2 minutes, and the Oedo Line walkway leads into the museum.
- Weekend caveat: the Sumo Museum is closed Saturday–Sunday; the Edo-Tokyo Museum is open (and open late Saturdays).
