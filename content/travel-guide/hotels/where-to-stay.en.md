The first decision for a Tokyo trip with Disney days isn't which hotel to book. It's **which base to use**: sleep next to the parks, or stay in central Tokyo and commute? Get this wrong and no amount of hotel research will fix the itinerary.

As the local guide here, we'll be honest: **staying in Urayasu is not always the right answer.** It depends on the shape of your trip.

## First, diagnose: what shape is your trip?

| Your trip | Recommended base |
|---|---|
| **2+ park days**, especially with kids | **Urayasu / Maihama** (from the night before your first park day) |
| **1 park day**, mostly Tokyo sightseeing | **Central Tokyo + day trip** (about 20 min from Tokyo Station) |
| 2 park days + several Tokyo days | **Split stay** (Urayasu for park nights, the city for the rest) |
| Late-night arrival at Haneda / Narita | **Urayasu from night one** (direct limousine bus, zero transfers) |

---

## Who should stay in Urayasu / Maihama

The essence of staying next to the parks: **your morning energy goes straight into the day.**

- **Make rope drop without pain** — from central Tokyo, lining up for opening means a 7am departure; next door, you walk (or shuttle) over after breakfast
- **Happy Entry** — Disney Hotel guests enter before general opening (**{{< fact "happy_entry.land_minutes" >}} min** at Land, **{{< fact "happy_entry.sea_minutes" >}} min** at Sea due to construction) ([how it works and which hotels qualify](/en/travel-guide/hotels/happy-entry/))
- **The nap reset** — when a small child crashes, you can go back to the room. Commuters can't copy this
- **Luggage is genuinely easy** — [delivery services from Maihama Station and the airports](/en/travel-guide/luggage/) mean you move hands-free
- **Bigger family rooms for the money** — rooms for 4–6 people are easier to find and better priced in Shin-Urayasu than central Tokyo ([family guide](/en/travel-guide/hotels/kids/) / [budget comparison](/en/travel-guide/hotels/budget/))

The honest downsides: **there is no nightlife**. After park closing your options are the hotel restaurant or a konbini. And every central-Tokyo sight is a 30–40 minute ride away.

## Who should base in central Tokyo

- **Only one park day**, with Asakusa, Shibuya or Ginza filling the rest
- **You care about dinner** — the city wins the evenings, overwhelmingly
- **Inventory and price competition** — especially for 1–2 travellers, central Tokyo often has more choice and better deals
- Maihama is **about 20 minutes from Tokyo Station** ([route details](/en/travel-guide/urayasu-maihama-access-guide/)) — for a single day, an entirely acceptable commute

The downsides are the edges of the day: rope drop means leaving around 7am, and staying to close means **kids asleep on the Keiyo Line home**. The Keiyo platform at Tokyo Station is also a 10-minute walk from everything — you'll feel it at night.

## The third answer: the split stay

For 2 park days plus several Tokyo days, **moving to Urayasu for the park nights** is genuinely the most comfortable pattern.

- **Order** — Tokyo-first or Urayasu-first both work. If unsure, arrange it so **no hotel move lands on a park-day morning** (arrive in Urayasu the evening before)
- **Luggage** — hotel-to-hotel courier forwarding is **next-day** as standard, so send ahead the day before. For a same-day move, use [the Sagawa counter at Tokyo Station](/en/travel-guide/luggage-airport/) (13:00 cut-off, delivers to Urayasu hotels)
- **Before check-in**, almost every hotel will hold your bags ([luggage guide](/en/travel-guide/luggage/))
